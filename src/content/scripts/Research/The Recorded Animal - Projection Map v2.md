## 1. THE UMBRELLA THESIS

**The Recorded Animal:** an inquiry into what happens to human beings when our every moment becomes potentially permanent — from the campfire to the cloud.

Every existing script and every future episode ladders to this thesis. Three things almost no one else is doing that you are positioned to do:

1. **Treat recording as a deeply human (not just technological) story.** You hold psychology, theology, anthropology, film history, business, and personal narrative in the same frame.
2. **Embrace the duality.** The camera is savior in one story and destroyer in the next. Resist collapsing the contradiction.
3. **Pull the camera back to the campfire.** Almost no one connects modern visibility behavior to ancestral storytelling instincts. That through-line is your moat — and it's now *live* in the trilogy ("your alarm was built for a village; we handed it the internet").

**NEW (v2):** The thesis now has a launched foundation — The Watched Brain trilogy (June 2026) — and a verified evidence bank in the Master Brief v2. Every Recorded Animal episode draws from and extends that bank: watching-eyes (Bateson 2006), objective self-awareness (Duval & Wicklund 1972), spotlight effect (Gilovich 2000), social facilitation (Zajonc 1965), thin slices (Ambady & Rosenthal 1993), looking-glass self (Cooley 1902).

## 2. SIX SERIES ARCS

### Series A — "Before the Lens"
*4 episodes · How we told ourselves to ourselves before there were cameras.*

- **Ep 1: The Handprint.** Sulawesi (~45,000 yrs), El Castillo (~64,000 yrs, likely Neanderthal), Cueva de las Manos, Chauvet (~30,000+). What was the audience? The future? The dead? Themselves?
- **Ep 2: The Campfire as Algorithm.** Homer, the West African griots, Aboriginal songlines. The fire selected memorable stories the way the algorithm selects shareable ones.
- **Ep 3: The Diary, the Letter, the Photograph.** Pepys. Anne Frank. The Brownie camera, 1900. What did we lose, and gain, when memory stopped being a story and became an image?
- **Ep 4: The Camera as Witness.** Zapruder, 1963. Rodney King, 1991. George Floyd, 2020. When did the camera stop being a luxury and become a moral instrument?

Hero prop: a handprint. Return to one in every episode.
Ladders into: Jevoy Palmer (foundational), Palmer House (depth play), MindYourBizniz (entrepreneurs as new storytellers).

### Series B — "Witness"
*6 episodes · The camera as observer, accomplice, and accuser.*

- **Ep 1: Why We Move the Camera at the Worst Part.** The deflection moment — the lens tilting away at impact. Embodied empathy bleeding through the device. (Verify the pattern with footage analysts and the Naudet brothers' 9/11 interviews before scripting as established fact — currently a strong observation, not a cited finding.)
- **Ep 2: The Schoolyard Fight, Recorded.** Bystander dynamics amplified by cameras. Interview a school counselor, a youth therapist. Cite the filming-vs-helping research carefully (see fact-check log).
- **Ep 3: Bodycams and the Watched Watchers.** Yokum, Ravishankar & Coppock (The Lab @ DC, 2017): no significant behavioral change. When does observation stop changing behavior? (Pairs perfectly with the Hawthorne-fade material already in the trilogy.)
- **Ep 4: The Capitol Self-Documenters.** January 6 rioters who livestreamed their own crimes. Being seen mattered more than not being caught. FBI archive is publicly searchable.
- **Ep 5: The Christchurch Stream.** 2019. Done with care, not sensationalism. The relationship between media and motive.
- **Ep 6: The Smile-You're-On-Camera Sticker.** Ring, Nest, the home as fortress. The "porch pirate" video as genre. (Watching-eyes study is the scientific anchor here — paper eyes, real behavior change.)

Hero prop: a phone screen tilted slightly off-axis.
Ladders into: Jevoy Palmer (signature), MindYourBizniz, documentary spinoff under YourBoyJevoy.

### Series C — "The Last Frame"
*5 episodes · People whose final moments were recorded — and what they recorded.*
The line: subjects are heroes, not spectacle.

- **Ep 1: ~127 Hours of Footage.** Aron Ralston, Blue John Canyon, April–May 2003. Filmed messages to family during the entrapment before self-amputating. The instinct to be witnessed may have kept him alive. Start: *Between a Rock and a Hard Place* (2004) + his TED talk.
- **Ep 2: Grizzly Man.** Timothy Treadwell **and Amie Huguenard**, October 2003. Audio of the attack exists; Herzog refused to use it. The camera as a barrier between Treadwell and his own danger.
- **Ep 3: The Pilot's Last Words.** Black boxes from Eastern 401 (Dec 1972) to Germanwings 9525 (Mar 2015). What does it mean that we know what dying people say?
- **Ep 4: The Lone Sailor.** Donald Crowhurst, Sunday Times Golden Globe Race, 1968–69. Falsified logs, tapes of his own unraveling. The recorded mind disintegrating in real time. Start: *Deep Water* (2006).
- **Ep 5: The Found Survivor.** Counterweight episode. Recordings that saved the recorder. The camera as guardian angel.

Hero prop: an SD card. A tape cassette. A small camera buried in snow.
Ladders into: Jevoy Palmer signature long-form, Palmer House Spotlight pillar.

### Series D — "Selfie Deaths"
*3 episodes · The most documented contradiction of our time.*

- **Ep 1: The Statistics That Should Have Made Us Stop.** Bansal et al. (AIIMS Bhopal, 2018): 259 deaths, 137 incidents, Oct 2011–Nov 2017, mean age ~23. India, then Russia, US, Pakistan. Drowning, transport, falls. Plus CMU/IIIT-Delhi's "Me, Myself and My Killfie" (2016). Walk through it clean-eyed. (Numbers have grown since 2017 — pull the latest count before scripting.)
- **Ep 2: The Cognitive Override.** Framing yourself in glass narrows peripheral threat detection. The phone suppressing the survival instincts that kept ancestors alive. (Inattentional blindness literature is the citable anchor here.)
- **Ep 3: The Larger Question.** The selfie death as the vivid edge of the phenomenon eating attention, relationships, presence. Don't moralize. Show the line.

Hero prop: the yellow selfie composition brackets, overlaid on Grand Canyon / train track / ocean cliff footage.
Ladders into: Jevoy Palmer signature. Strong algorithm performer.

### Series E — "Mirror With Memory" (anthology, ongoing)
*5–10 min episodes · Small recorded moments and what they did to us.*
(The title is canon now — the line lives in the trilogy.)

The wedding video became a film shoot · the birth recording — who is it for? · the funeral livestream · the first date, screenshotted to the group chat · the recital watched through a screen from the front row · the protest filmed by everyone · the vacation that became a vlog · the dinner that became content · the therapist's couch, now sometimes a TikTok · the deathbed goodbye, FaceTimed in.

Hero prop: a different small handheld object per episode.
Ladders into: Reel pillar of Palmer House. Highly producible at volume.

### Series F — "The Audience That Doesn't Exist Yet"
*4 episodes · How we perform for hypothetical future watchers.*
The through-line connecting most directly to the trilogy.

- **Ep 1: The Audience Inside Your Head.** Cooley's looking-glass self, 1902. We've been imagining audiences forever. The camera just gave them a body. (Direct sequel to the trilogy's "infinite, invisible, permanent eyes.")
- **Ep 2: The Future Boss / Future Date / Future Hiring Committee.** Growing up assuming everything will be reviewed. The cost of never being allowed a private adolescence.
- **Ep 3: The Posthumous Audience.** The dead now leave more documentation than ever. What does grief become when the dead can still be heard?
- **Ep 4: The Ghost in the Algorithm.** Performing for a non-human audience that pretends to know you. What does that do to a self?

Hero prop: a single empty chair. A microphone with no one behind it. The audience as absence.
Ladders into: the central Jevoy Palmer thesis.

## 9. THE INVESTIGATIVE METHOD (house style — now enforced via the Twelve Laws + skill file)

1. Find the specific moment first. Not the concept. The moment.
2. Name the mechanism — named research, dated study, cited thinker.
3. Honor the counterargument. The skeptic gets dignity — and real evidence — before they get dismantled.
4. Refuse the easy moral. Camera as savior in one frame, weapon in the next.
5. End on a question the audience can't shake.
6. Use objects. Props that argue, never decorate.
7. Verse capstone — matched to the theme, never repeated across a trilogy.
