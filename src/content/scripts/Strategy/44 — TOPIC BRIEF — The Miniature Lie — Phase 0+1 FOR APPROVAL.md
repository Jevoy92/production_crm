## PHASE 0 — THE TOPIC BRIEF

**TOPIC:** Your eyes don't report reality — they report a *best guess*, and the guess trusts context (lighting, perspective, comparison) over truth. Which is why a two-foot foam road can read as a canyon highway, and why your brain fails scale tests every single day.

**WHY IT'S INTERESTING:** Visual illusions are the most demonstrable phenomenon in the entire catalog — every act of the video IS the proof, happening to the viewer in real time. It's pure wonder (Law 13's favorite topic), it's Hollywood history, and it ladders to a real insight: perception runs on context, and whoever controls the context controls the read.

**FACT-CHECK NOTES ON THE SEED:**
- ⚠️ "Holzman & Rousey 1890 roots" — wrong citation (Holzman & Rousey 1966 is the voice-confrontation study). The room illusion is **Adelbert Ames Jr., 1946** — the Ames Room. Forced perspective as a craft predates film (theater, architecture — the Statue of Liberty's torch arm, Disneyland's Main Street upper floors are built at diminishing scale ✅ documented).
- ✅ The mechanism framing is right: the brain prioritizes lighting/context cues over raw size data. The formal name: **size constancy** — the visual system "corrects" retinal size using assumed distance, and the assumption can be hacked.

**THE EVIDENCE (verified):**
- 🏆 **The Ames Room (Adelbert Ames Jr., 1946):** a distorted trapezoidal room viewed through a peephole — two people of identical height appear giant and tiny. The brain chooses "normal room, impossible people" over "weird room, normal people," because right angles feel more trustworthy than human scale. Used in *Lord of the Rings* for hobbit scenes.
- **Size constancy + the Ponzo illusion (1911):** identical objects read as different sizes when context implies distance.
- **The moon illusion:** the horizon moon looks enormous; photograph it and it's tiny — the oldest mass forced-perspective event in human experience, still debated by scientists, free B-roll every month.
- **Adelson's checker-shadow illusion (1995):** two identical gray squares read as wildly different shades because the brain "corrects" for a shadow. The cleanest proof that you see *interpretations*, not pixels.
- **The Dress (February 2015):** the most viral color-constancy event in history — millions of people, two realities, one JPEG. Proof the brain's assumptions differ *person to person.*
- **Shepard's tables (Roger Shepard):** two identical tabletops that read as completely different shapes. Printable, traceable, undeniable on camera.

**REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES (named, verifiable):**
- *Lord of the Rings* — Gandalf and Frodo in the same cart via forced-perspective rigs that physically moved when the camera moved; Elijah Wood often acted to a tennis ball.
- *Elf* (2003) — Jon Favreau shot Buddy-among-elves almost entirely with forced perspective instead of CGI.
- Disneyland Main Street — second and third stories built progressively smaller so the street reads taller and cozier.
- Statue of Liberty / classical architecture — scale tricks centuries before cameras.
- Tourist photos pinching the Leaning Tower of Pisa — forced perspective as the people's art form.
