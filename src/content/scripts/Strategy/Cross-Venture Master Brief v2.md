## 1. VOICE & RULES (ALL VERSIONS) — unchanged core, sharpened

- Jevoy Palmer voice: casual, warm, "bro," "come on now," "you feel me?" Discovery frame ("I found this"). One strong landing line, not triple anaphora.
- NO fictional anecdotes or fabricated scenes. Ground everything in real psychology, real observation, real cited science.
- **NEW — No soft numbers.** Never "costs you about thirty percent of your bandwidth... there's no exact number." If a number can't be cited, cut it. Specific, textured, citable numbers only (259 deaths, 100ms, 3x the tin).
- NO inflated claims. Jevoy is launching. "Years of watching people freeze on shoots I directed" is banned phrasing until it's true. Say the honest version out loud — it differentiates: "We're a young company. I'm not going to claim we've filmed five hundred founders."
- Faith arrives last, quietly, as a reframe — never announced. **NEW — match the verse to the theme.** Don't default to Genesis 1:27 in all three versions. The Watched Brain trilogy used Psalm 139 (being fully seen) and Proverbs 18:16 (the gift makes room). One verse per script, load-bearing, never repeated across the trilogy.
- Props that argue, not decorate. One hero prop per script minimum.
- The skeptic gets dignity before they get dismantled. **NEW — arm the skeptic with real evidence** (e.g., Zajonc: audiences DO improve simple performance), then resolve with better evidence, not better framing.
- Each script: ~2,500 words, chapter-structured, teleprompter-ready (or read-ready for podcast).
- Body/location/props are part of the script (kitchen, garage, car, porch, walking, studio).
- **NEW — Every script passes the Twelve Laws** (see `00 — Script Blueprint`) before it's marked final. Non-negotiables: cliché kill in first 30s, spine promise with the wildest tease last, one accumulating device, a time/scale-shift act, re-hook at every chapter seam, flip that absolves before it re-empowers, identity close.

## 2. THE HOOK ASSEMBLY (ALL VERSIONS)

Felt moment (viewer's life, last-week familiar) → explicit cliché kill ("this is not the video that tells you to ___") → 2–3 part spine promise, most curiosity-loaded promise LAST, anchored to a concrete object ("there's a study with a coffee tin..."). 45–60 seconds, 120–160 words.

## 3. VERSION REGISTERS

### Jevoy Palmer Version
- Format: Cinematic investigative monologue, 9–11 min
- Opening: Quiet Universal — a felt experience framed as a shared secret
- Tone: "I want to investigate this" — curiosity-driven
- Props: Demonstrated on camera, visual cues in [brackets]; one accumulating device per script (index-card case file, list, board)
- Counterargument: "The skeptic" — intellectual, armed with real research
- Practical: every fix must trace to a named mechanism (fix ↔ cause, or cut it)
- CTA: Soft authority — "If that question lives inside you too, stay close"
- Music: none until the Chapter 5 flip, then score the insight
- Chapters: 7 (Felt Moment, Mechanism, Evidence/Time-Shift, Skeptic, Flip, Practical, Insight+Identity+Verse)

### Palmer House Productions Version
- Format: Cinematic brand monologue, 9–10 min
- Opening: The founder's freeze — Contrarian Swap hook in business terms
- Tone: "Here's what this costs your business" — strategic; cost must be *quantified* (100ms judgments, thin-slice research, the silent invoice)
- Props: same objects, framed as business tools
- Counterargument: "The strongest objection" — respectful but firm; concede what's true, show where the logic breaks (silence ≠ neutral; authored vs. unauthored)
- Practical: what Palmer House actually does — the four pillars (System, Reel, Evergreen, Spotlight) + the 1-to-12 body-of-work defense
- Launch-stage honesty stated on camera as a trust move
- CTA: "Book a strategy call. It's a real conversation, not a sales call dressed up as one."
- Stage cues: crew becomes visible in the practical chapter — production quality as proof
- Positioning lines (canon): "A translation company that happens to own cameras." "We help businesses become easier to understand, trust, and choose — through strategic cinematic video." "You bring what's true. We make it visible."
- Chapters: 7 (Founder's Freeze, Mechanism in Business Terms, Evidence + the Invoice, Strongest Objection, Flip, What Palmer House Does, Invitation+Verse)

### MindYourBizniz Version
- Format: Solo podcast monologue, 14–16 min — no props demonstrated, no scene cuts
- Opening: Cold open, room tone, mug set down, plain confession of the question
- Tone: "Let me sit with this" — intimate, slow; **NEW — this version takes the HUMAN altitude**, not the business altitude (see §4)
- Props: described verbally only ("imagine two objects on a table")
- Counterargument: "The friend who pushes back" — warmer, more personal, still armed with evidence
- The Emotional Chapter (Ch 6): the most personal stretch; one sustained music pad here only
- CTA: relational — "If that's the kind of conversation you've been needing, stay close"
- Production cues: [COLD OPEN], [BEAT], [PAUSE], [MUSIC FADES IN/OUT], [ROOM TONE]
- Host outro (canon): "Thanks for being here. MindYourBizniz is a Palmer House conversation. New episode next week."
- Include: Clip-worthy lines section (4–6), guest expansion options
- Chapters: 7 (Cold Open + Felt Moment, Mechanism, The Image You Hold in Your Mind, Friend Who Pushes Back, Flip, Emotional Part, Invitation+Verse)

## 4. THE ALTITUDE RULE (NEW — fixes the trilogy overlap problem)

One phenomenon, three altitudes. Never two scripts at the same altitude:

| Channel | Altitude | The question it asks |
|---|---|---|
| Jevoy Palmer | The mechanism | *Why are humans like this?* |
| Palmer House | The business cost | *What is this costing your company?* |
| MindYourBizniz | The inner life | *What does this do to a person's heart?* |

If the Palmer House and MindYourBizniz drafts could swap opening scenes without anyone noticing, one of them is at the wrong altitude. Rewrite it.

## 5. THE VERIFIED EVIDENCE BANK (cite these; add to this list as new ones are verified)

| Finding | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Objective self-awareness — observation splits you into actor + monitor | Duval & Wicklund, 1972 | The core mechanism |
| Watching-eyes effect — photo of eyes ≈ tripled honesty-box payments | Bateson, Nettle & Roberts, 2006, *Biology Letters* (note: replications debated — say so) | Hero evidence; hero prop |
| Hawthorne Effect — being studied changes behavior; effect fades with familiarity | Western Electric, 1924–1932 (original data debated — say so) | History act + "record until boring" |
| Social facilitation — audiences improve simple tasks, impair complex ones | Zajonc, 1965, *Science* | Arms the skeptic, then resolves |
| Spotlight effect — we overestimate observers ~2x (Barry Manilow shirt) | Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, 2000 | Cuts the imagined crowd in half |
| 100ms trust judgments; confidence rises with exposure, verdict doesn't change | Willis & Todorov, 2006 | Palmer House cost case |
| Thin-slice judgments — 30s silent clips predict semester-end ratings | Ambady & Rosenthal, 1993 | Body-of-work defense |
| Voice confrontation — bone conduction makes your recorded voice sound wrong only to you | Holzman & Rousey, 1966 | "Don't trust day-one playback" |
| White coat hypertension — observation alone moves blood pressure | AHA clinical literature | Proof it's physical |
| Selfie deaths — 259 deaths, 137 incidents, Oct 2011–Nov 2017, mean age ~23 | Bansal et al., 2018, *J Family Med Prim Care* (AIIMS Bhopal) | Recorded Animal, Series D |
| Looking-glass self — the self is built from imagined reflections | Cooley, 1902 | Audience That Doesn't Exist Yet |
| Front stage / backstage | Goffman, 1959 | Identity chapters |
