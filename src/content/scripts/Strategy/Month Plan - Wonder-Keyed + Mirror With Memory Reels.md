# The Month — Four Phenomena, Wonder-Keyed
*Four core phenomena, each run through all three altitudes, plus Mirror With Memory reels threading underneath. All evidence already banked — no research sprint needed. Flips rewritten per the Wonder Rule (Law 13): the downer is the doorway, not the room.*
*Note: Aron Ralston / "The Witness" trilogy is already built (files 17–19) — that's this month's savior-side anchor, already in hand.*

## Week 1 — Seen but not known (Goffman, 1959) — **BUILT: files 22–24**
- **JP: "The Mask Is a Costume, Not a Prison"** — visibility and knownness come apart; why we bond with presentations and miss persons. **Wonder flip:** Goffman wasn't writing a tragedy — if all the world's a stage, then the role is *yours to write.* Kids know this; dress-up is joy. You get to choose what you author, and you get to take the costume off.
- **PH: "Recognized Isn't the Same as Trusted"** — a brand can be everywhere and still never chosen, because the visible layer is generic and unauthored. **Wonder flip:** authorship is the fun part — your brand gets to be a *character* the market can actually know. Character design, not compliance.
- **MYB: "Known by Thousands, Seen by No One"** — the loneliness of maintaining a self you don't recognize. **Wonder flip:** the backstage isn't hiding — it's the playroom. Guard one room where the costume comes off, and the front stage becomes a game you play instead of a debt you service.

## Week 2 — The audience that doesn't exist yet (Cooley, 1902)
- **JP: "You're Performing for People Who Aren't in the Room"** — identity has always been built from imagined reflections; the feed gave the imagined crowd a body. Hero prop: a single empty chair. **Wonder flip:** the imagined audience is also where imagination *lives* — rehearsal, dreaming, the kid narrating their own adventure to nobody. Children perform for invisible audiences constantly and it's joy, not surveillance. The chair can hold a critic or a coach, a mob or a grandmother. You're the casting director. Reclaim the chair.
- **PH: "Building for the Client You Don't Have Yet"** — founders who price, message, and build for a hypothetical investor or dream logo while ignoring the real customer in front of them. **Wonder flip:** imagined audiences are a *design tool* when chosen deliberately — write for one named, real-shaped person and the hypothetical mob loses its vote. The "one reader" discipline as creative freedom.
- **MYB: "The Self You Never Get to Put Down"** — never being allowed a private, un-audienced version of yourself, and what it costs your rest. **Wonder flip:** un-audienced hours are where the self regrows — sabbath as the original backstage. Play without witnesses is not wasted; it's the soil.

## Week 3 — Judged before you speak (Ambady & Rosenthal 1993; Willis & Todorov 2006)
- **JP: "They Decided in 100 Milliseconds"** — the verdict forms before you open your mouth; more exposure raises confidence without changing the verdict. Hero prop: stopwatch / muted clip. **Wonder flip:** you don't lose the snap judgment — you get to *author the first frame.* That's not a sentence to await; it's the most concentrated creative real estate you own. Directors, hosts, and grandmothers setting a table all know it: the opening shot is hospitality. A gift you give, not a verdict you fear.
- **PH: "The First Thirty Seconds Already Cost You the Deal"** — thin-slice research means the silent first impression of your video IS the pitch. Strongest business week of the month. **Wonder flip:** thin slices are *cheap to design.* Light, warmth, the first three seconds of a real smile — tiny authored choices, outsized verdicts. The smallest art with the biggest return.
- **MYB: "Being Read Before You're Heard"** — what constant instant assessment does to a person. **Wonder flip:** you read people too — and your snap warmth is a gift you're constantly giving without knowing it. The liking gap research says strangers like you more than you think. The courtroom is friendlier than the defendant assumes.

## Week 4 — The sound of your own voice (Holzman & Rousey 1966) — *the light one, on purpose*
- **JP: "Why You Hate Your Own Voice on Playback"** — bone conduction; the recorded voice sounds wrong only to you, and only on day one. Most relatable, most shareable hook of the month. **Wonder flip (already playful):** stop flinching — it's just skull acoustics. Everyone else has only ever heard recording-you, and they like that person fine. Your voice is fine. Go make the thing.
- **PH: "The Founder Who Won't Watch the Footage"** — self-playback avoidance silently kills content output; ties to "record until it's boring." **Wonder flip:** watching your own footage is a skill with a hilariously short learning curve — day three you stop seeing the cringe and start seeing the craft. The fastest taste upgrade in the business.
- **MYB: "Learning to Recognize Yourself"** — the gap between the self you hear inside and the self the world hears. **Wonder flip:** the world's version of you has been received with more warmth than your inner version predicts — playback as reconciliation, not exposure. Make friends with the stranger in the recording; everyone else already did.

# Mirror With Memory — Reel Scripts (short-form engine, run all month)
*30–60s each. Palmer House Reel lane. One small handheld object per reel. Two rueful, two joyful, one balanced — per the Wonder Rule, the anthology runs both directions.*

## Reel 1 — "The Recital" (rueful → tender)
[Vertical. Jevoy, close, quiet energy.]
Front row of a recital, a father watches his daughter's whole solo on a four-inch screen. She's forty feet away — live, full color, once. Here's the science part: a 2014 study found we remember *less* of what we photograph — the brain files it under "the machine has it" and lets go. He'll keep the file forever and almost never open it. **The phone keeps the pixels. Only you can keep the moment.** One song per show — hands empty, eyes up. Film the rest. That one's yours.

## Reel 2 — "The Wedding" (balanced)
[Vertical. B-roll of a wedding shoot, Jevoy VO or to-camera.]
Somewhere in the last twenty years, the wedding video stopped documenting the day and started *competing* with it — couples staging a film on the one day they swore to be most present. And listen, I film these — beautiful work, worth doing. But here's the line we hold on set: the camera serves the day; the day never serves the camera. If a moment has to be re-staged for the lens, it already happened without it — and the real one was better. **Document the wave. Don't direct the ocean.**

## Reel 3 — "The Dinner That Became Content" (rueful, funny)
[Vertical. A plated dish. A phone descends into frame above it.]
Nobody eats first anymore — the phone eats first. And fine, the pasta's photogenic. But watch what happens to the table when one rectangle comes out: every conversation pauses, posture shifts, the room goes front-stage — we covered the science, paper eyes change behavior, real lenses more so. The dinner becomes a set. **You can have the content or you can have the table. The wise ones decide per meal — on purpose.** Tonight: let the kitchen win one.

## Reel 4 — "The Grandmother Tape" (joyful — savior side)
[Vertical. An old hand, a kitchen, warm light. Jevoy close.]
Here's the camera being the hero. Somebody finally sat grandma down and hit record — the story she's told a hundred times, the laugh in the middle of it, the way she says certain words. Twenty minutes on a Tuesday. And in thirty years that file is the most valuable thing the family owns — the voice, not the photo, the *voice.* **Some recordings take you out of the moment. This one rescues the moment from time itself.** You know exactly who to film. Do it this month.

## Reel 5 — "First Steps" (joyful — savior side)
[Vertical. A kid watching a phone, delighted. Jevoy VO.]
A four-year-old watching the video of her own first steps — pointing at the screen going "that's ME." That's not screen time, bro — that's a human meeting her own story for the first time. The handprint on the cave wall, 45,000 years later: *I was here. I happened.* We talk a lot on this channel about what cameras cost us. **This is what they're for** — handing someone the proof that their life was witnessed, and loved, from the start.
