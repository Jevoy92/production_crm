# SCRIPT SYSTEM v3 — The Operating Overview

The single source of truth for how scripts get made across Jevoy Palmer, Palmer House Productions, and MindYourBizniz. Supersedes the workflow portions of the Blueprint (00), Master Brief v2 (05), and Skill (06) — those get refactored to match this doc once it's approved. **Status: DRAFT FOR JEVOY'S MARKUP — no scripts get written until this is signed off.**

## 1. WHAT THIS SYSTEM PRODUCES

One topic at a time — a phenomenon worth investigating (something that affects humans in some interesting way, especially where it crosses psychology) — turned into three standalone pieces: one per venture, each with its own angle, its own title, its own format, its own CTA. Plus the 1-to-12 clip map per script.

The pipeline, in order, with an approval gate between each phase:

| Phase | Output | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Topic Brief | One page: the phenomenon, the verified evidence, the three angles | Jevoy approves topic + angles |
| 1. Titles & Packaging | 3–5 scored title candidates per venture + thumbnail direction + format choice per script | Jevoy picks titles + formats |
| 2. Scripts | Three full scripts, written to the approved briefs | Jevoy reviews voice + content |
| 3. Finishing | QA pass (checklist below), teleprompter versions, clip maps | Ship |

**Never start at the script. The script is the last thing that happens, not the first.**

## 2. THE CONSTITUTION (hard rules — these never bend)

**The Standalone Rule.** Every script is written for someone who has never seen anything else we've made. ZERO callbacks: no "as we covered," no "this week's flagship," no "recall our earlier investigation," no references to other episodes, channels, trilogies, or series structure. If a concept is needed, it gets explained fresh, inside this script, in two sentences or less. Test: if this is the first thing a stranger ever watches, does any sentence make them feel like they walked in late?

**No fabrication. Ever.** No invented DMs, relatives, conversations, habits, or "last week" events. The honest toolkit: real public verifiable stories · explicitly hypothetical scenes ("picture this") · second-person scenes ("you know that moment when…") · composite truths framed as such ("a conversation I keep having") · the recording moment itself · Jevoy's real standing facts (launch-stage builder, Jamaican-born, Seattle/Renton, production company, hosts The Next Step weekly live Q&A).

**Kitchen-table voice.** Sounds like talking to a close friend: long looping spoken sentences mixed with short ones, self-interruptions, digressions that circle back, real humor (2–3 laugh moments minimum), named real-world examples (2–3 minimum), science delivered like gossip ("So it's 1956, right, and these two researchers start noticing something weird…"). Punchy lines earned, one per beat, never stacked. Ration "bro" — seasoning, not a tic. Read-aloud test: if he couldn't say it gesturing with a coffee mug, rewrite it.

**Launch-stage honesty.** No implied veteran status, no inflated client counts, no soft numbers. "Here's what I'm building/learning/found." Stated honesty is a differentiator, not a weakness.

**Science owned, not rented.** Named researcher, year, real finding. Debates acknowledged (e.g., Hawthorne's original data, watching-eyes replications). If it can't be verified, it doesn't go in.

**The skeptic gets dignity** — the strongest version of the counterargument, armed with real evidence, conceded where it's right. (Principle, not a mandatory character — see §4.)

**The Wonder Rule.** The turn lands in play, wonder, or creative power — never merely "how to manage the danger." Camera as savior AND destroyer across the body of work; never mine only the destroyer.

**Verse capstone.** Every script ends with scripture — matched to the theme, never repeated across the three scripts of one topic, arriving quietly, never preachy. This is the one fixed ritual in the whole system.

## 3. PHASE 0 — THE TOPIC BRIEF (one page, every time)

```
TOPIC: ____________  (the phenomenon, stated in one plain sentence)
WHY IT'S INTERESTING: the curiosity hook — what makes a stranger lean in
THE EVIDENCE: 3–6 verified items (researcher, year, finding) + 1 hero
  piece of evidence (the coffee-tin-grade item the whole topic hangs on)
REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES: 3+ named public stories/examples available for use
THE THREE ANGLES (one phenomenon, three doors):
  JEVOY PALMER  — the human mechanism: "why are we like this?"
  PALMER HOUSE  — the business cost/opportunity: "what is this costing you?"
  MINDYOURBIZNIZ — the inner life: "what does this do to a person's heart?"
FORMAT CALL: which structure each script wants (see §4) and why
VERSES: three candidates, one per venture, theme-matched, no repeats
CTA: locked primary per venture + any secondary this topic earns (see §5)
RISK/CARE NOTES: anything needing reverence (real victims, tragedy,
  politically charged material) and how we'll handle it
```

Example of the level of focus required: not "cameras and psychology" — that's a series, not a topic. The topic is "why people get awkward the moment a camera points at them." One phenomenon. Three doors into it.

## 4. STRUCTURE — A SPINE, NOT A SKELETON

### The Default Spine (four movements, not seven chapters)

Most scripts will move through four jobs, in roughly this order, with total freedom in how many beats each takes:

1. **OPEN** — earn the next minute. A felt moment from the viewer's own life, a real public story, or a cold question. Kill the expected cliché ("this is not the video that tells you to ___"). Promise something specific worth staying for.
2. **INVESTIGATE** — the why. The evidence, delivered conversationally, with the named examples doing the heavy lifting. Curiosity escalates; each beat opens the next door.
3. **TURN** — the reframe. The moment the viewer's relationship to the phenomenon changes. Absolve first (it's the mechanism, not you), then re-empower, landing in wonder/play per the Constitution.
4. **LAND** — make it matter. What to actually do, or what to actually feel. Identity close (who you become), verse, one CTA.

### Named Alternate Formats (the topic brief picks; the spine doesn't get to be a rut)

| Format | Shape | Use when… |
|---|---|---|
| The Case File | One true story told beginning-to-end; the science woven in as the story needs it | One real story is strong enough to carry the whole topic (an Aron Ralston, a Kevin Carter) |
| The Fair Fight | Two genuinely opposed bodies of evidence argued at full strength; resolution earned late | The honest science actually cuts both ways and pretending otherwise would be dishonest |
| The Countdown | Numbered list with escalation logic (weakest → strongest) | The topic is naturally plural: myths, rules, mistakes, signs |
| The Experiment | Jevoy tests the thing on himself / on camera; results structure the episode | The phenomenon is demonstrable in real time |
| The Letter | Direct address to one specific kind of person, start to finish | An intimate topic where "you" is one person, not an audience (MYB-friendly) |
| The Field Piece | Shot / told from a real location; place drives structure | The phenomenon lives somewhere visitable |

### Anti-template rules

- No two consecutive episodes on the same venture use the same format.
- Recurring devices — "the friend who pushes back," the accumulating prop, the music-at-the-flip — are tools in a box, not requirements. If the last episode used the personified friend, this one handles the counterargument differently.
- If a topic demands a shape not on this list, **invent the shape.** The list serves the topic, never the reverse.

## 5. THE THREE VENTURES (angle, register, CTA)

| | JEVOY PALMER | PALMER HOUSE | MINDYOURBIZNIZ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angle | The human mechanism — curious investigation of why we're like this | The business translation — cost, opportunity, and what to do about it | The inner life — what it does to a person's heart; sit-with-it |
| Medium | Cinematic video, props / visuals argue | Cinematic video, business framing, crew / production visible as proof | Audio-first monologue; no props shown, everything described |
| Length | 2,500+ spoken words, minimum (≈ 12–16 min depending on edit pace) | 2,500+ spoken words, minimum (≈ 12–15 min) | 2,500+ spoken words, minimum (≈ 15–18 min) |
| Primary CTA (locked) | "If this question lives in you too — stay close." + subscribe | "Book a strategy call. It's a real conversation, not a sales call dressed up as one." | "Send this to the one person who needs it." + follow |
| Secondary CTA (optional, set in topic brief) | The Next Step (free Friday live Q&A) · a download · comment prompt | The Next Step · portfolio / portal · a specific lane offer | The Next Step · a journaling / practice prompt |
| Faith register | Verse last, quiet | Values language; verse last, quiet | Most personal; verse last |

Palmer House positioning canon (always available, never mandatory): *"A translation company that happens to own cameras." / "You bring what's true. We make it visible."*

## 6. TITLES & PACKAGING (Phase 1 rules)

- Titles are designed **per venture, from the topic** — three different doors, not one title in three fonts.
- The proven pattern from our ViDIQ testing (scores 80–94): **[startling specific fact] + [personal stakes]** — "Half the people in this study never saw the gorilla. That's why selfies kill." (94). Topic-descriptive titles scored 56–80. Lead with the finding or the felt experience, never the category.
- Title names the tension; thumbnail shows the felt moment; never repeat one in the other. Browse > search for this genre — package for curiosity.
- Every candidate scored in ViDIQ before locking; target 75+. Ethics check: the hook is never a victim (heroes, not spectacle).

## 7. PHASE 3 — THE QA CHECKLIST (every script, before it ships)

1. **Standalone check** — would a first-time viewer ever feel they walked in late? (Search the draft for: "we covered," "last week," "earlier episode," "this series," "as I said before.")
2. **Fabrication check** — every personal anecdote either true, explicitly hypothetical, or honestly composite. Every study real and citable.
3. **Template check** — does this share its skeleton beat-for-beat with the previous episode on this venture? If yes, restructure.
4. **Read-aloud check** — kitchen-table test on every paragraph; quote-card-stack test (no aphorism pileups).
5. **Humor check** — 2–3 genuine laugh moments. **Example check** — 2–3 named real-world examples.
6. **Wonder check** — does it end bigger, lighter, or more curious than it began?
7. **Launch-stage check** — no inflated authority anywhere.
8. **Word count** — 2,500 spoken words minimum, every script, every venture. Count spoken words only (cues, headers, and notes don't count). Going over is fine; coming under fails QA. **Clip map** — 8–12 standalone segments marked.
9. **CTA** — one primary, correctly matched to venture; secondary only if the brief assigned one.

Then and only then: teleprompter version generated (words only, no cues, no blank lines).

## 8. WORKED EXAMPLE — Phase 0 + 1 ONLY (no script, per the new pipeline)

**TOPIC:** Why people get awkward the moment a camera points at them.
**WHY INTERESTING:** Universal, felt this week by everyone, and the real explanation (it's ancient machinery, not a personality flaw) genuinely surprises.
**EVIDENCE:** Duval & Wicklund 1972 (objective self-awareness — the watcher/actor split) · Bateson, Nettle & Roberts 2006 (paper eyes ≈ tripled honesty-box payments — hero evidence) · Zajonc 1965 (audiences help simple tasks, wreck complex ones) · Gilovich 2000 (spotlight effect — imagined audience ~2x real) · Holzman & Rousey 1966 (why your recorded voice sounds wrong only to you) · Hawthorne fade (observation effects die with familiarity).
**REAL EXAMPLES:** Zoom self-view fatigue (Bailenson, Stanford 2021) · karaoke (car-Whitney vs. stage hostage situation) · wedding-toast freeze · athletes who choke on camera vs. perform live.

**THREE ANGLES + CANDIDATE TITLES** (to be ViDIQ-scored at the gate):

- **JP (mechanism):** the ancient alarm meets the lens — "A photo of eyes made people pay 3x more. That's why you freeze on camera."
- **PH (business cost):** the freeze is killing founder content; it's a production problem, not a courage problem — "You're great in meetings and terrible on camera. Here's the mechanism."
- **MYB (inner life):** the watched self vs. the real one; who gets to meet the unguarded you — "Why being seen feels like danger."

**FORMAT CALLS:** JP = Experiment (demonstrate the freeze on camera, live) · PH = default spine · MYB = Letter (to the founder who deleted the take last night).
**VERSES:** JP — Psalm 139 (fully seen, called wonderful) · PH — Proverbs 18:16 (the gift makes room) · MYB — 1 Samuel 16:7 (man looks at the outward appearance).
**CARE NOTES:** none — light topic.

That's the artifact that gets approved before a single script line is written.

## 9. WHAT HAPPENS TO THE EXISTING ~30 SCRIPTS

They're inventory, not waste — but every one needs a retrofit pass before filming: standalone violations stripped (the cross-references), voice pass where still aphoristic, fabrication sweep. That happens per-topic as each one comes up in the calendar — retrofit at the moment of use, not as a big-bang rewrite. The fixed / teleprompter folders get regenerated only after retrofit.
