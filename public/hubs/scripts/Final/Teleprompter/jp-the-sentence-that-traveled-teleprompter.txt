Second witness. And this one is almost rude how fast it works.

2006. Two researchers at Princeton — Janine Willis and Alexander Todorov. They showed people photos of faces. Strangers. And they flashed them for a tiny slice of time. A tenth of a second. One hundred milliseconds.

A hundred milliseconds. That's faster than a blink. And in that sliver, people had already decided things. Is this person trustworthy? Are they competent? Do I like them? Snap. Done. Verdict in.
